# Intaglio Travel Poster

Create one **independent 16:9 horizontal intaglio study poster per uploaded photograph**.

The result should feel like a premium, fictional security-print specimen: finely engraved, low-saturation, paper-based, collectible, and clearly derived from the original photograph — but **never like real currency**.

## 1. First principle: preserve the photograph

Faithfulness to the uploaded photo has higher priority than decoration.

Preserve as accurately as possible:

- subject identity;
- number of people, buildings, vehicles, bridges, boats, towers, trees and other major objects;
- pose, gesture and orientation;
- relative scale between major subjects;
- camera viewpoint and horizon;
- perspective and vanishing direction;
- skyline silhouette;
- landmark geometry;
- terrain and water relationships;
- foreground / midground / background depth;
- lighting direction;
- time-of-day atmosphere;
- major color relationships.

Allowed:

- restrained crop to fit 16:9;
- minor edge extension when needed for framing;
- subtle simplification of tiny background clutter;
- slight tonal compression so engraving lines remain readable.

Do not:

- mirror the image;
- rotate the scene;
- move landmarks;
- invent a new skyline;
- add buildings, towers, mountains, bridges, boats, trees or people that are not present;
- remove visually important subjects;
- redesign the original architecture;
- change the main viewpoint;
- distort the proportions of major structures.

The finished image must be immediately recognizable as the same place, person, building, or scene.

## 2. Canvas and composition

- Final ratio: **16:9 horizontal**.
- One source image = one final poster.
- The original scene must occupy most of the canvas.
- Security-print decoration should wrap around, sit behind, or occupy existing negative space.
- Do not shrink the scene into a small central picture surrounded by an oversized ticket frame.
- Do not force every image into the same decorative layout.

Use the source image's composition as the layout engine.

**Core rule:** decoration must obey the photograph; the photograph must never be rearranged to obey a template.

## 3. Intaglio engraving language

Translate the entire photograph into a refined fictional intaglio / security-print engraving.

Use:

- extremely fine parallel engraving lines;
- curved contour engraving;
- cross-hatching;
- nested directional hatching;
- stippling;
- dot engraving;
- micro-line shading;
- line-density based tonal modeling;
- restrained ink buildup in the darkest areas.

For architecture:

- façade divisions;
- window grids;
- rooflines;
- masonry joints;
- bridges;
- road edges;
- railings;
- towers;
- domes;
- pagodas;
- skyline contours

should be rebuilt through precise linework rather than broad painterly shading.

For landscapes:

- hills;
- tree crowns;
- clouds;
- water;
- rock;
- shoreline;
- foliage masses

should be described with directional engraved line systems rather than soft digital blur.

For people:

- preserve facial proportion, hairstyle, clothing silhouette, pose and identity;
- use delicate engraved contour lines for facial planes;
- build hair through directional micro-lines;
- build clothing folds through curved hatching;
- keep faces readable and human rather than over-textured.

## 4. Light and tonal construction

The image should read like real intaglio printing.

Use:

- paper as the brightest tone;
- sparse lines for highlights;
- medium line density for midtones;
- dense cross-hatching and stippling for shadows;
- slight relief-shadow impression around dark printed strokes.

Avoid:

- airbrushed gradients;
- glossy 3D rendering;
- photographic blur;
- charcoal smudging;
- thick woodcut blocks;
- heavy comic outlines;
- watercolor wash;
- oil-paint texture.

## 5. Color system

Use **3–5 low-saturation print colors** derived from the source photograph.

Base paper colors may include:

- warm white;
- gray-beige;
- old ivory;
- pale sand;
- light warm gray.

Ink colors may include:

- pale cyan;
- blue-gray;
- muted teal;
- faded green;
- slate blue;
- ochre;
- warm tan;
- burnt sienna;
- brick red;
- rust red;
- deep blue-gray.

Preserve one or two source-specific accent colors when they define the scene, for example:

- a red tower;
- orange roofs;
- illuminated pagoda edges;
- autumn foliage;
- warm bridge lights;
- blue night sky;
- warm window light.

Accent colors must remain integrated into the engraving system, not behave like neon digital overlays.

Do not force the same palette onto every photo.

## 6. Day / night adaptation

### Day scenes

Prefer:

- warm ivory paper;
- pale blue-gray sky engraving;
- subdued ochre / brick / faded green accents;
- visible paper highlights;
- softer overall density.

### Dusk scenes

Prefer:

- gray-beige or warm ivory paper;
- muted steel-blue or teal linework;
- selective amber / rust accents;
- restrained contrast between sky and lit architecture.

### Night scenes

Do not simply paste dark photography onto paper.

Instead:

- translate dark sky into dense blue-gray engraving;
- convert light sources into warm printed accents;
- keep dark areas legible through line density rather than flat black;
- preserve reflections, road lights and skyline illumination as engraved warm-ink details.

Night scenes should remain recognizably nocturnal while still looking printed on paper.

## 7. Security-print ornament

Add only abstract, fictional, non-governmental security-print decoration.

Allowed:

- guilloche spirals;
- intertwined fine rings;
- elliptical loops;
- wave-security patterns;
- radial line fields;
- fine rosettes;
- micro-line grids;
- abstract geometric filigree;
- short fictional security threads;
- pale watermark-like shapes;
- delicate registration marks;
- very slight color misregistration.

Placement should respond to the image.

Good locations include:

- open sky;
- empty water;
- quiet corners;
- edge bands;
- low-detail negative spaces.

Do not place large ornaments over important architecture, faces, landmark silhouettes, or critical scene details.

### Variation rule

Never repeat the exact same:

- flower;
- rosette;
- large sun disk;
- corner flourish;
- circular motif;
- guilloche arrangement

in the same place across every output.

Every poster should feel like a unique engraved plate.

## 8. Paper and physical print texture

Use subtle, premium physical realism:

- fine cotton-paper fibers;
- slight natural paper tooth;
- gentle warm aging;
- minimal edge wear;
- slight absorbent ink edges;
- microscopic ink relief;
- pressed intaglio shadow;
- very light plate-pressure impression;
- tiny local ink irregularities;
- restrained registration drift.

Avoid:

- dirty parchment;
- pirate-map aging;
- large coffee stains;
- burned edges;
- extreme scratches;
- heavy distressing;
- fake antique damage.

The paper should look archival and tactile, not ruined.

## 9. Typography

Add location text in the **lower-left corner** by default unless the source composition requires a nearby alternative.

Use the most accurate English place or landmark name available.

Examples:

- `TOKYO`
- `FLORENCE`
- `WUHAN`
- `CHONGQING`
- `WEST LAKE · HANGZHOU`
- `GULANGYU · XIAMEN`

Typography should:

- be uppercase or restrained small caps;
- use an engraved serif / classical publication character;
- remain readable at preview size;
- feel printed into the same plate rather than digitally overlaid;
- stay subordinate to the scene.

A smaller secondary line may contain a country, district, or landmark qualifier only when useful.

## 10. Specimen text

Only a very small amount of fictional specimen text is allowed.

Preferred examples:

- `SECURITY STUDY 02`
- `INTAGLIO SAMPLE`

These should remain small and peripheral.

Do not add dense fake documentation text.

## 11. Location accuracy

Location naming must be conservative.

Priority:

1. use the user's explicit location label;
2. use an unmistakable landmark when confidence is high;
3. otherwise use a broader confirmed city or region;
4. if the location is uncertain, omit overly specific text rather than guessing.

Do not mislabel a city, landmark, district, or country.

## 12. Multiple-photo rule

When multiple images are uploaded:

- process each independently;
- one source photo = one output poster;
- do not merge different cities or trips;
- do not create a collage grid;
- do not combine multiple locations into one fictional landscape unless the user explicitly asks for it.

## 13. Strong prohibitions

Never generate:

- real currency denominations;
- fake spendable banknotes;
- real bank names;
- real government agencies;
- real issuing authorities;
- official state emblems;
- real national seals;
- official coats of arms;
- signatures;
- realistic serial numbers;
- machine-readable codes;
- QR codes;
- barcodes;
- brand logos;
- official watermarks;
- copied banknote layouts;
- copied security patterns from an identifiable real banknote;
- text such as `AI GENERATED`, `ORIGINAL PHOTO`, or model labels.

Never imitate a specific existing banknote.

Do not create something that could plausibly be mistaken for circulating currency.

## 14. Style prohibitions

Avoid:

- cyberpunk neon;
- anime;
- cartoon styling;
- watercolor;
- oil painting;
- glossy vector art;
- generic photo filters;
- black-and-white pencil sketch;
- crude woodcut;
- thick comic contours;
- decorative scrapbook stickers;
- travel-ticket collage;
- postage-stamp layout;
- random tourist icons;
- fake official seals;
- excessive ornamental clutter.

## 15. Master generation prompt

Use this as the core prompt pattern:

> Transform the supplied photograph into one independent **16:9 horizontal premium intaglio travel / portrait / architecture study poster**.
>
> The source photograph is the absolute structural reference. Preserve the exact identity and number of major subjects, architecture, landmark geometry, skyline silhouette, camera viewpoint, perspective, horizon, spatial relationships, foreground / midground / background depth, lighting direction and recognizable color relationships. Do not mirror, move, remove or invent major buildings, people, bridges, boats, vehicles, mountains, trees or landmarks. Do not rebuild the scene into a different cityscape. Allow only restrained cropping or minor edge extension to fit 16:9.
>
> Translate the whole scene into a **fictional security-print / banknote-inspired intaglio engraving**, without copying any real currency. Reconstruct architecture, people, landscape, water, clouds and roads using extremely fine parallel engraving lines, curved contour hatching, cross-hatching, stippling, dot engraving and micro-line tonal fields. Highlights should expose the paper; midtones should use moderate line density; shadows should be built from dense engraving and subtle ink relief. Build a restrained palette of **3–5 low-saturation print inks derived from the source photograph**, such as pale cyan, blue-gray, muted teal, faded green, ochre, warm tan, brick red, rust or deep blue-gray. Preserve only the source's most identifying accent colors as restrained printed highlights.

> Add the accurate English location or landmark name in the **lower-left corner** in restrained engraved serif capitals. If useful, add one much smaller qualifier line. Allow only minimal fictional specimen text such as `SECURITY STUDY 02` or `INTAGLIO SAMPLE` in a peripheral area.
>
> The finished work must still be immediately recognizable as the original photograph, but should look as though the scene was newly engraved and multi-color printed as an archival fictional security-print specimen.

> Never generate real denominations, bank names, government agencies, signatures, serial numbers, national seals, official emblems, QR codes, barcodes, logos, official watermarks, copied real-banknote patterns, or any layout that could be mistaken for circulating currency.

## 16. Quality check

Before finalizing, verify:

- Is the aspect ratio 16:9 horizontal?
- Is the output clearly based on the same source photograph?
- Are all major landmarks and subjects still in the correct positions?
- Is the camera viewpoint preserved?
- Is the original perspective preserved?
- Is the skyline or architectural silhouette accurate?
- Were any extra landmarks or structures invented?
- Are engraving lines fine and directional rather than painterly?
- Are highlights created through exposed paper?
- Are shadows created through line density?
- Is the palette derived from this specific source?
- Is the security ornament abstract and fictional?
- Does decoration avoid covering important subjects?
- Does this poster avoid repeating the exact same ornamental template used on previous outputs?
- Is the location label accurate?
- Is text minimal and readable?
- Does the result avoid all real-currency identifiers and denominations?
- Could the result be mistaken for real circulating currency? If yes, regenerate with more obviously fictional specimen styling.

If any critical answer fails, regenerate with stronger source-preservation constraints.
